# Prefix-only harmonic forecasting (AN-04D)

Analyzer owner: `src/analyzer/HarmonicForecast.ts`; contract: `src/types/harmonicForecast.ts`. This is deterministic, environment-independent research DSP. Playback, worker messages, TrackAnalysis, state, visuals and XR charts are unchanged. No dependency was added.

`fitHarmonicForecast(prefix, prefixIdentity)` accepts exactly one original-clock AN-04B pitch window N=next power of two >=80ms. Its identity hashes the prefix alone and carries its own timeline offset. PitchCandidates estimates periodicity on this prefix, then the new code fits two fixed hypotheses using two-pass QR: constant harmonic coefficients, and coefficients varying linearly with normalized time. Each has a DC term and up to eight harmonics below0.4Fs. No source label, teacher pitch, suffix or model selection enters fitting. The result contains both coefficient sets and training residuals, or explicitly unavailable status.

`evaluateHarmonicForecast(forecast, suffix)` tests the immediate next N/2 samples without updating the model. For a clip window beginning at sample s: training support is [s,s+N), evaluation support [s+N,s+1.5N). The suffix energy is centered using the prefix mean. Error/energy can exceed one; near-zero evaluation energy yields null. The ratio is neither probability nor calibrated musical confidence. Numeric QR rank rejection uses relative column norms, not a promised global SVD condition-number equivalence.

This removes AN-04C's full-window-frequency dependency: its coefficient holdout was conditional on frequency estimated using the whole window. AN-04D's window is longer and its predictive errors are therefore not directly comparable to AN-04C conditional errors. Neighboring windows overlap and their observations are correlated. The suffix is future relative to its prefix, not independent unseen music or a statistical confidence sample.

Linear harmonic envelopes can express gradual amplitude changes. Independent signed cos/sin coefficient slopes also allow local quadrature/phase variation: their improvement on a stationary tone may partly absorb prefix F0 estimation error, without improving the exported pitch estimate. They can extrapolate poorly on frequency modulation and mixtures. Both hypotheses stay visible; a small training residual or favorable suffix never automatically promotes a model. A future production selector needs prefix-internal validation or a separate calibration set, followed by held-out evaluation. Changing frequency, transients and nonlinear envelopes need separate hypotheses and falsification controls.

All results retain `confidence:null`, `sourceIsolation:unverified-mixture`, `consumerUse:diagnostic-only`, `xrScoring:ineligible`. Octave/unison mixtures can predict well; a single vibrato source can predict badly. Neither outcome supplies voice count, an isolated instrument, a persistent musical entity or a motif. Semantic layers may eventually use validated acoustic observations for dramaturgy and choreography; this experiment does not publish them as authoritative cues or gameplay targets.

References are task-specific: actual NumPy/SciPy agree on sampled linear-system arithmetic; actual librosa/audioFlux separately analyze isolated sampled prefixes. Their two-vote25-cent pitch agreement remains a pseudo-label and does not validate source identity or a forecast. Both comparisons retain disagreements, versions and hashes. Recorded music lacks musical truth and gets no accuracy percentage.
